Knowledge & Guide

Having slow-release supplements made — the complete overview.

What does slow-release mean for supplements?

The term slow-release (also sustained-release or delayed release) describes a dosage form in which an active ingredient is released not all at once, but gradually over an extended period. Instead of a brief spike right after intake, you get a more even release over hours. That is precisely the goal of a good slow-release supplement.

Technically, this is achieved through special coatings, matrix systems or depot capsules that release the active ingredient in a controlled way. This demanding galenics is far from something every manufacturer offers — it requires know-how, the right equipment and experience with formulation.

For a private brand, that is exactly the advantage: anyone who works with a contract manufacturer that has mastered delayed active-ingredient release can offer a product that many cheap standard capsules on the market simply can't match — a genuine differentiator in a crowded segment.

Why delayed active-ingredient release can make sense

Some active ingredients are absorbed by the body quickly and excreted just as fast. Delayed active-ingredient release can help spread the supply more evenly across the day, instead of a brief spike followed by a rapid drop. Especially with water-soluble active ingredients that the body doesn't store, a release distributed over several hours is a sensible approach.

For a private brand, that is a strong argument: a sustained-release formulation or depot capsule visibly sets your product apart from simple standard capsules and underlines the premium character of your line. Well-considered galenics is also easy to explain in your communication — a plus that builds trust.

Depot capsules, sustained-release and matrix — the methods

Several methods are possible for contract manufacturing. Depot capsules release their contents step by step. Sustained-release tablets use a matrix from which the active ingredient is slowly dissolved out. And with micro-pellets, release can be controlled especially finely, because many small units with different coatings can be combined.

Which method is right depends on the active ingredient, the desired release duration and the dosage form. We advise you individually and choose the technique that suits your product.

The right balance is key here: release should be delayed but reliable, so the desired effect is maintained over time. This fine-tuning is the real craft behind a good slow-release formulation — and exactly where the experience of a specialised manufacturer pays off.

Enteric coating and bioavailability

Closely related to slow-release is enteric coating. Acid-sensitive active ingredients — such as probiotics or certain plant compounds — would be destroyed in the stomach. An enteric coating carries them safely into the small intestine, where absorption takes place.

This can improve the bioavailability of sensitive active ingredients. Slow-release and enteric coating can also be combined — for example as an enteric-coated depot capsule with delayed release in the intestine. Ultimately, both techniques aim at the same thing: that the active ingredient arrives where it's needed and isn't lost unused. Which combination makes sense for your product depends on the specific active ingredient — we check this as part of formulation development.

What is the difference between slow-release and sustained-release?
The terms essentially mean the same thing: a delayed active-ingredient release distributed over hours, instead of a brief spike. Sustained-release is the older term, known mainly from pharmaceuticals, while slow-release is the common English label.
