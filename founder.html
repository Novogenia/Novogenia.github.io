Founder & CEO

Dr. Daniel Wallerstorfer.

Austrian molecular biologist and biotechnologist. Since 2009 he has built Novogenia into one of Europe’s leading laboratories for genetic analysis, personalized supplements and cosmetic technologies — and today drives the innovation across the listed Novogenia AG group.

The idea

Turning the genome into everyday health.

Daniel’s conviction is simple: the two metres of DNA inside every cell hold the most personal health information a person will ever have — and almost none of it was being used. He set out to change that, building a company that reads the genome and turns it into something tangible: a supplement matched to your biology, a cosmetic formulated for your skin, a prevention plan built on your own genetic risks.

To make that possible at scale, Novogenia became one of the very few companies in the world to own the entire chain in-house — the genetic laboratory, the supplement production, the cosmetics manufacturing and the software that ties them together. “Personalization only works,” Daniel argues, “when you control every step from the DNA to the finished product.”

Background & career

From Manchester to Salzburg.

Born in Salzburg in 1982, Daniel studied Molecular Biology at the University of Manchester and completed a Ph.D. in Biotechnology by the age of 25 — his research focused on recombinant protein expression, RNA interference and epigenetics. In 2009 he returned to Austria and founded Novogenia with private investors, creating one of Europe’s first dedicated preventive genetic testing laboratories.

  • 2009 — founds Novogenia; secures the Austrian licence for medical genetic tests
  • 2011–12 — builds a network of 100+ partner labs; acquires Genosense Diagnostics
  • 2013 — company turns profitable; adds DNA-based personalized supplements, later cosmetic genetics
  • 2020 — parent company Novogenia AG (formerly Darwin AG) is listed on the Munich Stock Exchange
  • Today — leads multiple group companies and a continuous R&D programme

DEEP GENOME

An artificial-intelligence system for automated genome interpretation — the engine for the next generation of deeper, faster genetic insight.

Science: Today there are already about 4 million scientific publications that have studied the effects of genes on the human body. That genes influence body weight, the effectiveness of certain strategies and the ability to handle certain nutrients is supported by multiple scientific studies for each gene — the genetic traits determined by our analyses are therefore considered scientifically confirmed.

Recommendations: The adaptations of micronutrient dosing, cosmetic formulation and dietary or lifestyle recommendations derived from these findings have not yet been confirmed by randomised, placebo-controlled studies for every genetic effect. They are therefore to be understood as logical conclusions — not scientifically proven outcomes — and do not replace medical advice, diagnosis or treatment.
